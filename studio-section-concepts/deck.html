“Stage it again”

The replacement for the home page’s before/after drag section. One empty room, a pool of 60 staged variations, and a button that deals a different one every press. The empty photo is Fal.ai flux/dev; every staged image is real Stagify output, generated by driving processStaging() against that exact photo with the prompts in lib/staging/promptMatrix.js.

Press the button repeatedly. Watch the label change on the first press, and watch for a repeat — there shouldn’t be one until you’ve been through all 60.

Decisions baked into this

  1. A shuffled bag, not Math.random() per press. Picking uniformly at random repeats fast — with 60 items there is roughly a 40% chance of a duplicate within the first eight presses, and one duplicate reads as “broken”, not “unlucky”. The pool is shuffled once and walked in order; on exhaustion it reshuffles, and the reshuffle is prevented from dealing the card already on screen.
  2. Nothing is prefetched. The section loads the empty room and stops. Each press fetches exactly one image and only swaps once it has decoded, so a visitor who never presses pays nothing for the pool existing. This is why 60 costs no more at load than 6 would.
  3. The stack is sized 1216×832, the source photo’s own ratio — not a rounded 3/2. Since the empty base and every staged card are the same photograph, a 2% ratio mismatch would make the furniture appear to shift when a card lands.
  4. The working state is a sheen, not a spinner. The wait is one image fetch over the network. A spinner would imply the render is happening live, which would be a lie — the pool is pre-generated.
  5. The copy never says “shuffle”. The product has no re-roll button; you re-run, and because nothing pins a seed on the main staging path the result genuinely differs. “Stage it again” is what the product actually does.
