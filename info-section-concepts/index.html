“What virtual staging is, and why it sells”

Seven ways to lay out the four info rows. Same copy, same four images, same content box width (1044px, matching .home-section) in every one — so the height badges are a fair comparison. Photos are placeholder SVGs.

Each concept is interactive. Try clicking, tabbing through with the keyboard, and narrowing the window to check the phone layout.

Today — four stacked rows

The current section, rebuilt here with the placeholder photos. This is the baseline every other height is measured against.

  • Four full row-heights of scroll
  • No interaction at all
  • Everything visible, zero JS

Measuring…

1

Accordion + persistent media pane my pick

All four headings stay visible and stacked on the left; opening one expands its body and bullets while the matching photo cross-fades into a fixed pane on the right.

  • All four h3s stay scannable
  • Photos stay a visible feature
  • Degrades to today's layout without JS
  • Needs a new script + CSS

Measuring…

2

Segmented tabs + split panel

A four-up pill control on top; below it the active row renders in today's image-beside-text shape.

  • Most compact of the two-column options
  • Familiar, obvious control
  • Three headings shrink to short tab labels
  • Echoes the 3D studio carousel further down the page

Measuring…

3

Native <details> stack — zero JS

The same pattern the FAQ section already uses. <details name> gives exclusive-open behaviour with no script at all; the photo lives inside the opened body.

  • No JavaScript, nothing to break
  • Smallest collapsed footprint here
  • Matches an existing repo pattern
  • Photos hidden until a row is opened

Measuring…

4

Expanding 2×2 card grid

Four compact cards in a grid. Clicking one promotes it to a full-width row with its photo and copy; the other three stay as thumbnails.

  • All four photos visible at once
  • Reads as a gallery, not another carousel
  • Layout shifts as cards reflow
  • Tallest of the compact options

Measuring…

5

Horizontal image strips

Four vertical photo panels side by side. The active one widens and reveals its copy over the image; the rest collapse to a spine with the title set vertically.

  • The most visually striking option
  • Fixed height — never reflows
  • Vertical titles are hard to read, and worse in German
  • Copy over a photo hurts contrast & a11y

Measuring…

6

Scroll-snap rail

A horizontal peek carousel: one card at a time with the next one edging in, native scroll-snap plus dots.

  • Native scrolling, tiny amount of JS
  • Excellent on touch
  • Only one heading visible at a time
  • Horizontal scroll is poor on desktop with a mouse

Round two — built around strips

7–10 keep what makes strips work (photos always on show, one panel widening, a fixed height that never reflows) and each fix a different weakness. 11–13 are new shapes.

7

Horizontal bands strips, rotated

Strips turned 90°. Four full-width photo bands stacked; the active one grows downward and reveals its copy. Same feel, but no text ever rotates — every title reads normally at every state.

  • Fixes the vertical-text problem outright
  • Titles stay readable in any language
  • Degrades to a plain stack on mobile
  • Copy still sits over a photo

Measuring…

8

Strips + glass copy card

The original strips, but the body and bullets land on an opaque white card instead of directly on the photo. Fixes the contrast problem without changing the shape.

  • Body copy is properly legible
  • Contrast no longer depends on the photo
  • Vertical spine titles still rotate
  • Card hides more of the active photo

Measuring…

9

Strips with wide spines

Collapsed strips get wide enough to hold a normal horizontal label, so all four headings are readable at all times and nothing rotates. The active strip takes about half the width instead of two thirds.

  • All four headings always readable
  • No rotated text anywhere
  • Less room for the active photo and copy
  • Tight for the longer language packs

Measuring…

10

Strips, auto-cycling

The original strips plus a 5-second auto-advance with a progress bar. Pauses on hover, pauses when focus is inside it, stops when scrolled off-screen, and does not autoplay at all under reduced motion.

  • Shows all four without any input
  • Motion draws the eye to the section
  • Content moving unprompted annoys some readers
  • Can swap out a panel mid-sentence

Measuring…

11

Hero + filmstrip

One wide 2:1 stage with the copy in a card over the left third, and a row of four labelled thumbnails underneath to switch. The most “premium product page” of the set.

  • Biggest, most impactful photo
  • Copy on an opaque card — good contrast
  • 2:1 crops a 4:3 photo hard
  • Only one heading visible at a time

Measuring…

12

Mosaic

An asymmetric grid: one large feature cell carrying the active photo and its copy, with the other three stacked beside it as tiles you can click to promote.

  • All four photos on screen at once
  • Compact and doesn't reflow
  • Copy card covers a lot of the feature photo
  • Weakest of the set at phone width

Measuring…

13

Chips + detail drawer

All four headings sit in a row of cards across the top; the selected one opens a drawer below with its photo and copy. Essentially concept 1 laid out horizontally.

  • All four h3s visible, none rotated
  • Copy on the page background — no contrast risk
  • Simplest of the compact options to build
  • Least visually interesting
