The Top 10 AI Virtual Staging Sites for 2026, Ranked

By Stagify.ai · July 22, 2026 · 10 min read

Virtual staging has gone from a niche add-on to something almost every listing gets — and there are now dozens of tools competing for the job. Most "best of" lists are just feature dumps. This one ranks the ten we think are genuinely worth your time in 2026, tells you what each is actually best at, and says plainly where the cheaper or slower option is the right call.

Full disclosure: this is Stagify's blog, and Stagify is on this list at number one. We think the reasoning below holds up on its own — but you should read it knowing we have a stake in it, and check the numbers yourself before you buy anything. Every price here comes from publicly listed pricing at the time of writing.

How we ranked them

Six criteria, weighted roughly in this order:

  • Cost at real volume. Not the headline price — what a 10-photo listing actually costs you, every week, for a year.
  • Speed. Seconds vs. days. This is the whole reason AI staging exists.
  • Control. Can you fix the one thing that came out wrong, or do you just re-roll and hope?
  • Output quality. Correct perspective, believable lighting, furniture that fits the room's actual scale.
  • Ownership & watermarks. Can you put the image on the MLS without a logo across the couch?
  • Learning curve. How long from signup to a photo you'd send a client.

1. Stagify.ai — best overall value and control

Stagify stages an empty room in about 8 seconds and costs a flat $11.99/month for unlimited photos on Stagify+, with a free tier to start. That pricing is the headline, but it isn't why it's first here — the control is.

Most AI staging is a slot machine: press the button, take what you get, press it again. Stagify adds two things almost nothing else in this list has. The Masking Studio lets you paint exactly which region of the photo may change and attach specific furniture to it, leaving every other pixel untouched — so you can fix one bad chair without re-rolling the whole room. The AI Designer lets you just talk to the room: "warmer lighting, swap the rug, lose the plant." We go through both in this deep dive.

It also covers eight room types — living room, bedroom, kitchen, dining room, bathroom, office, outdoors, and a new Dorm mode — outputs with no watermark, and leaves you full copyright on every image.

Where it isn't the answer: if you need exactly one hand-crafted image for a $4M listing and want a human designer's judgment on it, a designer-led service (below) is still the better buy.

Best for: anyone staging more than two or three photos a month.

2. Virtual Staging AI — fastest one-click workflow

One of the most established AI-first tools, built around speed: upload, pick a style, get a staged photo in roughly 15 seconds, with unlimited regenerations on paid plans. Entry pricing sits around $25/month for a small image allowance, so the per-photo math gets worse if you stage in volume.

The interface is about as simple as this category gets, which is genuinely a feature — there's very little to learn. The trade-off is the usual one: control is mostly limited to style presets and re-rolling.

Best for: agents who want the fewest possible clicks and don't need to fine-tune the result.

3. BoxBrownie — the human-edited standard

BoxBrownie isn't AI at all, and that's the point. Real editors stage your photo by hand, typically at around $24 per image for 2D virtual staging with a 24–48 hour turnaround. Quality is consistently high because a person is making the decisions, and the wider catalogue — item removal, day-to-dusk conversions, floor plan redraws — makes it a one-stop shop for listing photography.

The cost is the cost. A 10-photo listing runs well into the hundreds, and you wait a day or two for it.

Best for: a small number of high-stakes images where you want human judgment and don't mind paying for it.

4. Styldod — designer-led, tuned for premium listings

Another human-in-the-loop service, with publicly listed pricing in the $16–$23 per image range — noticeably cheaper than BoxBrownie while keeping the designer-led model. Output leans photorealistic and is a common pick for luxury and higher-end listings, where a slightly-off reflection or a floating rug is fatal.

Same structural trade-off as any human service: you're buying quality with money and time, not getting it instantly.

Best for: luxury listings that need a hand-finished look at a friendlier price than the premium services.

5. REimagineHome — staging plus renovation visuals

REimagineHome is broader than staging alone: it does interior redesign, exterior and landscaping concepts, and renovation "what-if" visuals as well as furnishing empty rooms. Plans span roughly $14 to $99/month depending on how many images you need.

If part of your pitch is "here's what this dated kitchen could look like," that range is worth real money. If you only ever furnish empty rooms, you're paying for surface area you won't use.

Best for: agents and investors selling potential, not just furniture.

6. Collov AI — the volume play

Collov's pitch is per-image economics at scale: annual plans that work out to roughly a quarter per image once you're using the full quota, plus selective furniture removal and free revisions. That's about as cheap as staged pixels get.

The catch is the shape of the commitment — the good rate depends on an annual plan and on actually burning through the credits. If you stage in bursts, a flat monthly plan is simpler and often cheaper in practice.

Best for: brokerages and photo teams with predictable, heavy monthly volume.

7. Decor8 AI — design-first, staging-capable

Decor8 comes at the problem from interior design rather than real estate, at around $14.99/month. It's strong at generating a lot of looks for a space quickly, which makes it a good idea generator — you can throw twenty styles at a room and see what the space wants to be.

Because the focus is design exploration, listing-specific niceties are thinner than in the real-estate-native tools.

Best for: homeowners and designers deciding on a direction, not just marketing a listing.

8. Apply Design — staging from your phone

Apply Design sells credits in small packs (roughly $7–$10) and is one of the more comfortable options to use on a phone, which matters more than it sounds — you're often standing in the empty room when you want the render. Turnaround is fast and the barrier to trying it is low.

The credit model is the drawback: it's fine for occasional use and gets expensive if staging becomes routine.

Best for: quick mobile staging during or right after a walkthrough.

9. PadStyler — genuine pay-as-you-go

PadStyler will sell you a single staged image from about $4.99, with subscriptions from roughly $29/month for 10 credits up to $99/month for 50. That per-image floor is the most useful thing about it: if you need one photo, once, you can buy exactly one photo.

Beyond a handful of images, though, the credit tiers land well above what flat-rate tools charge for unlimited output.

Best for: one-off images with no subscription attached.

10. Spacely AI — concept generation for designers

Spacely starts around $39/month and is aimed squarely at design workflows — generating and iterating on interior concepts rather than pushing listing photos out the door. If your output is a moodboard or a client presentation, that framing helps.

For pure listing staging it's the most expensive entry point on this list, which is why it sits at ten rather than reflecting anything wrong with the product.

Best for: interior designers presenting concepts to clients.

What actually separates the top of this list from the bottom

Three things, consistently:

  • Pricing model beats price. A tool at "$0.25 per image" can cost more per year than a flat $11.99/month plan, depending entirely on how you actually work. Do the math on a typical month, not on the headline.
  • Control is the quality ceiling. Every model in this category produces the occasional broken chair. The tools that let you fix just that chair end up producing better final images than the tools with a slightly better first draft.
  • Watermarks and ownership are not details. A staged photo you can't put on the MLS is not a staged photo. Check the terms on free tiers specifically.

And whichever you pick: disclose it. Virtual staging is widely accepted on the MLS, but the rules about labelling it are real — we cover them in this guide to MLS disclosure.
