Every staged photo on an MLS has to say it was staged. Ours says it before you download it.

Here is the whole job:

1. Tick "Label as virtually staged" in the staging panel.
2. Pick the look. A dark chip, a light chip, text only, or a band across the foot of the photo.
3. Drag the size if you want it louder.

That is the feature. There is no step four.

The tag is burned into the file, not laid over the preview, so it survives the download, the email and the re upload. And if the tag cannot be applied, you do not get the image. That is on purpose. A photo you believe is labelled and is not is the exact problem the tag exists to prevent.

The dining room here is a real render. The table, the chairs, the rug, the pendant and the print were all generated. The room was empty. The tag in the corner went on by itself.

NAR Article 12 and most MLS rules ask for the same thing: if a listing photo has been altered, say so. Nobody is asking for an essay. Two words in the corner is enough.
