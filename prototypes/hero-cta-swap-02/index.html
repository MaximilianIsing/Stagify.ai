Hero CTA street pan: cleaner handover

"Street pan is basically perfect but the actual transition over needs a little bit of work, like it's not perfectly clean, so go for a new generation with that in mind."

21 September 2026. Same street pan, seven ways to hand the old house over to the new one. Pick a style chip in any card to play its swap.

Ranking

Scored on fit to the brief (30), distinctiveness (20), build cost (20), mobile and accessibility (15), first-glance pull (15).

    Only houses travel, the road rolls under them, nothing crosses the label.

  4. 06Two Beat 84%B

01

Street Pan

B- 80%

The baseline: every band slides at its own depth, sky stays. Watch the stars and the lamp mid-pan.

  • Reuses the hover parallax bands as they ship
  • Reads as one continuous camera move
  • Under a second
  • Two star fields overlap while the far band pans
  • Lamp and tree sweep under the label
  • House crosses the background house mid-pan
02

Clean Plate

A- 90%

Only the two houses travel. Sky, skyline, road, lamp and tree are pinned, so nothing can ghost.

  • Nothing overlaps, nothing crosses the label
  • Two animated groups, cheapest pan here
  • Lamp glow stays put on hover
  • Reduced motion fallback is a dissolve of two groups
  • Houses glide while the ground stays, slightly floaty
  • Less sense of travelling than the full pan
03

Rolling Road

A- 92%

Houses travel and the road dashes roll with them; lamp, tree and sky are pinned like foreground props.

  • Road motion grounds the houses, no floaty feel
  • Dash offset animates on one path, no seam
  • Nothing crosses the label or doubles up
  • Same bands and easing the hover already uses
  • A pinned lamp on a moving street is a small cheat
  • Dash roll needs its own reduced motion switch
04

Street Memory

B+ 87%

The current house shrinks back into the background slot as the new one arrives, so your last pick stays on the street.

  • Continuous street, the pan lands exactly on the next scene
  • Background house becomes meaningful, not decoration
  • Handover is a single motion, no crossing
  • Scene builder must take two styles, not one
  • Scale and slide must land on the exact mid slot
  • First paint needs a chosen background house
05

Motion Blur

D 64%

Full pan with a horizontal blur that peaks mid-transit, so the crossover is too fast to read.

  • Feels fast and expensive
  • Crossover flaws are invisible under the blur
  • SVG filter repaints a 470px plate every frame
  • Blur on a moving plate is a vestibular trigger
  • Masks the problem instead of removing it
  • Label loses contrast against smeared lights
06

Two Beat

B 84%

The old houses leave fully, the street is empty for a beat, then the new ones arrive. Never two houses at once.

  • Old and new never share a frame
  • Exit and entry can use different easings
  • Simple to reason about, easy to interrupt
  • Total time creeps past a second
  • The empty beat can read as a stall
  • Two timers instead of one animation
07

Short Slide

C 74%

A 48px nudge plus a dissolve, half a second. Clean, but the street stops being a place.

  • Fastest here, 0.5 seconds
  • Trivial to build and to interrupt
  • Works as its own reduced motion fallback
  • Houses ghost through each other during the dissolve
  • Reads as a UI transition, not a camera
  • Lowest pull of the set
08

Van Delivery

C+ 78%

The removal van drives across and drags the new street in behind it, a masked wipe.

  • Reuses the van the hover already draws
  • Strongest story of any variant
  • People click chips just to watch it
  • Collides with a hover van already parked
  • Needs a registered property or a rAF loop
  • 1.3 seconds, slow by the fifth click
