01

Split Proof

Pick this if you believe the product sells itself and the words are getting in the way. The before/after drag is the hero — full-bleed, edge to edge — and the copy lives on top of it instead of above it. You trade the safety of a clean readable headline block for a first frame that makes the value literal in under a second. The risk is real: text over photography needs a heavy scrim to stay legible, and that scrim is doing contrast work your current flat blue background does for free.

+ Proves the product before it explains it + One idea, not four stacked ones + Drag interaction invites a first touch + Reuses assets you already ship − Text-over-image contrast is fragile across 11 locales − Full-bleed image is your LCP; budget gets tight
MEDIA 2 hi-res paired shots, art-directed for a left-side text safe zone JS ~1.5 KB pointer drag; works static if JS fails (fixed 50%) MOBILE Scrim flips to bottom-up, copy stacks under the seam I18N Headline safe zone must survive German (+35% length) ≈ same height as today, ~4 fewer text blocks

02

Dropzone First

Pick this if the hero’s only job is to get a file into the app. It deletes the marketing hierarchy entirely — no eyebrow, no announcement pill, no six-word headline — and puts the actual upload target at the optical centre of the page, big enough to drag onto without aiming. This is the highest-conversion shape for people who already know what you do, and the worst shape for people who don’t: a cold visitor from Google gets a box and very little reason to trust it. The thumbnail strip underneath is the compromise that buys back a little context.

+ Shortest possible path to first render + Real drag-and-drop target, not a button that opens one + Cheapest to build and to translate − Says almost nothing to a first-time visitor − Weak for SEO: little indexable above-fold copy
MEDIA 5 small thumbs, ~120px — no hero image at all JS Drag/drop + file input you already have; ~0 new weight MOBILE Dropzone degrades to a tap-to-choose card; strip wraps A11Y Must stay a real <input type=file> under the box ≈ 40% shorter than today’s hero

03

Style Sentence

Pick this if you want the hero to be a toy. The headline is an unfinished sentence and the style chips finish it — tap “Coastal” and both the word and the room change together, so the visitor discovers the feature set by playing rather than by reading a list. It reframes you from “a button that stages a room” to “a room you can direct,” which is a materially different (and more defensible) product story. Cost: you’re preloading six full-size renders above the fold, and the whole idea collapses to a static image if a chip is never tapped.

+ Teaches the style range in the first 3 seconds + Interaction has a payoff, not just a hover state + Headline stops being a slogan and becomes a control − 6 above-fold images: real bandwidth cost on mobile − Sentence-with-a-slot grammar breaks in some languages
MEDIA 6 same-room renders (you already ship exactly these) JS ~1 KB toggle; decode-on-idle for chips 2–6 MOBILE Stacks image-over-copy; chips become a scroll rail I18N Highest risk here — slot position moves per language 6 styles surfaced above fold vs 0 today

04

Editorial Ledger

Pick this if you want to look like an established company rather than a tool. No hero image at all: one enormous left-aligned typographic statement, then a hard rule, then four numbers on a ledger row. It’s the fastest-loading option on the page by a wide margin and the only one where the LCP element is text you control completely. The tradeoff is that it asks the visitor to take your word for it — a staging product that shows no staged room above the fold is a strange thing, and the numbers have to be genuinely good or the whole frame reads as bluffing.

+ Fastest LCP available; text-only above fold + Reads senior; separates you from AI-tool sameness + Zero art direction, zero image maintenance − Shows no product; visual proof is pushed below fold − Lives or dies on whether the four stats impress
MEDIA None — no hero image, no carousel JS Only the existing stat counter animation MOBILE Ledger becomes 2×2; headline clamps to ~34px I18N Safest of all eight — plain block copy ≈ 55% shorter than today, ~0 KB hero media

05

Three-Beat Filmstrip

Pick this if your real conversion problem is disbelief, not awareness — people don’t doubt that you exist, they doubt it’s actually one click. The hero becomes the whole process laid out as three lit panels that auto-advance, so “upload → choose → done” is shown as a sequence rather than claimed as a slogan. It’s the most explanatory option and therefore the most patronising to a returning user, and it’s the one most likely to feel like a template if the panel art isn’t distinctive.

+ Kills the “is it really that easy?” objection up front + Auto-advance gives motion without a video + Each panel is a natural i18n unit − Feels like an onboarding slide, not a homepage − Mobile can only show one panel; sequence weakens
MEDIA 3 panel images + 1 UI screenshot JS ~1 KB interval; pauses on hover and on reduced-motion MOBILE One live panel at a time + dots; taller than desktop A11Y Needs a pause control — auto-advancing content ≈ 25% taller than today’s hero

06

Sticky Command Bar

Pick this if you want to shrink the hero rather than redesign it. The top of the page becomes a short, quiet statement — no pill, no eyebrow, no stat block — and the CTA detaches into a bar that stays with the visitor for the whole scroll. It’s the only variant that makes the rest of your homepage the point, which matters if the sections below (why-us, testimonials, restage) are doing your actual persuading. It also permanently spends ~60px of every viewport, and floating bars are the first thing users learn to ignore. Scroll inside the frame below to see it hold.

+ CTA reachable from any scroll depth + Smallest hero; content below gets seen sooner + Lowest-risk change to your existing page − Occupies viewport permanently, especially on mobile − Collides with cookie banners and the language pill
MEDIA None in the hero; imagery moves below the fold JS Zero — pure CSS position:sticky MOBILE Bar goes full-width bottom; must clear iOS safe-area Z-INDEX Known conflict: language pill sits at z-index 1000 ≈ 65% shorter hero; CTA visible 100% of scroll

07

The Wall · THE SWING

Pick this if you want the homepage to feel like a place where a lot is happening. A tilted, slowly drifting mosaic of real staged rooms fills the entire viewport behind a four-word headline, with a live ticker of just-completed renders along the bottom. It’s the only variant that communicates scale — you have a hundred-image pool sitting in the repo doing nothing — and it’s the one a cautious designer would talk you out of, because it is heavy, it is loud, and continuous background motion is exactly the pattern accessibility guidelines are wary of. Worth prototyping precisely because it can’t be arrived at by editing what you have.

+ Communicates volume instantly; nothing else here does + Uses the existing 100-render pool as the design + Ticker turns your render log into live social proof + Memorable — the one people describe to someone else − Heaviest option: many images + perpetual animation − Needs a real reduced-motion path and a mobile fallback
MEDIA 18–24 small tiles from the existing restage pool JS Ticker copy only; drift is CSS transform (compositor-only) MOBILE Drop to 3 columns, freeze drift, ticker becomes static A11Y Hard requirement: prefers-reduced-motion stops all drift ≈ same height, ~20× the imagery of today

08

Ask First

Pick this if you want the hero to qualify the visitor instead of pitching them. It opens with a question, not a claim; answering it reveals a tailored second line and only then the upload. You learn the room type before the file arrives, which means a better first render and a segmentation signal you currently get for free from nobody. The cost is a deliberate extra step in front of your primary conversion — every tap you add between arrival and upload is a place people leave — and a dark hero is a big departure from the light-blue identity you’ve run for a year.

+ Captures room type before upload → better first result + Question framing outperforms slogans for attention + Answer text is a natural place to preempt objections − Adds a tap before the primary action − Dark hero breaks the established light-blue identity
MEDIA None above fold — gradient only JS ~1 KB; skip-link straight to upload for repeat visitors MOBILE Excellent — chips wrap, reveal keeps the fold short I18N 6 room labels; they already exist in your packs ≈ 30% shorter than today, +1 interaction step
